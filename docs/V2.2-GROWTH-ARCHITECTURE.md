# Content Factory V2.2 — Growth Architecture Contract

## Status

This document defines the canonical V2.2 growth-domain architecture. V2.2 extends the certified V2.1 production engine; it does not replace or casually rewrite V2.1 execution, retry, concurrency, lease-fencing, artifact, validation, or publication semantics.

## 1. Mission

Content Factory V2.2 is a multi-brand growth operating system that converts market evidence and business goals into measurable content, funnels, leads, conversions, and reusable learning.

The system must optimize for business outcomes rather than raw content volume or vanity metrics.

## 2. Top-level ownership model

```text
Workspace
  |
  +-- Brand
       |
       +-- Product
       |    |
       |    +-- Market
       |    +-- Audience
       |    +-- Offer
       |    +-- Funnel
       |
       +-- Opportunity
       +-- Campaign
            |
            +-- Content Series
            |    |
            |    +-- Content Item
            |
            +-- Production (V2.1 engine)
            +-- Experiment
            +-- Distribution
            +-- Performance
            +-- Attribution
            +-- Learning
```

A workspace may own many unrelated brands. Brand knowledge, assets, audiences, offers, campaigns, performance and learning must never leak across brands unless an explicit cross-brand relationship is created.

## 3. Separation of responsibilities

V2.2 has three cooperating planes.

### Growth plane

Determines what should be produced and why:

```text
Market Signals
  -> Opportunities
  -> Strategy
  -> Audience / Offer / Funnel
  -> Campaign / Series
  -> Content Hypothesis
```

### Production plane

The existing V2.1 pipeline remains canonical:

```text
SIGNAL -> IDEA -> BRIEF -> BIBLE -> CONCEPT -> SCRIPT -> SHOT_PLAN
-> ASSET_PLAN -> ASSETS -> EDIT -> PLATFORM_ADAPTATION -> VALIDATION
-> PUBLISH -> ANALYZE -> LEARN
```

### Measurement plane

Determines whether production created useful outcomes:

```text
Publication
  -> Performance Events
  -> Visits / Sessions
  -> Leads
  -> Conversions
  -> Attribution
  -> Experiment Evaluation
  -> Learning
  -> Strategy Update
```

## 4. Brand Brain

Each brand has durable, versionable strategic context. At minimum:

- identity and positioning
- mission and value proposition
- products and offers
- audience definitions
- pains, desires and objections
- tone of voice
- visual language
- claims policy and prohibited claims
- competitors and reference sources
- reusable characters, locations and assets
- successful and failed creative patterns
- accumulated learnings

The Brand Brain is not one mutable mega-prompt. Runtime context must be assembled from explicit structured records and immutable/versioned artifacts where appropriate.

## 5. Product, market, audience and offer

A Product is the thing being promoted or grown.

A Market defines geographic/language/commercial context.

An Audience defines a target segment and its awareness/problem state.

An Offer defines the promised exchange and intended conversion action.

Campaigns must be attributable to these entities so the factory can learn which combination works.

## 6. Opportunity engine

An Opportunity is an evidence-backed candidate for action. It may originate from search behavior, social trends, competitor content, comments, reviews, forums, first-party analytics, customer questions, seasonality, news, or historical factory performance.

Every opportunity must support:

- source/evidence references
- observation timestamp
- confidence
- relevance to brand/product/audience
- expected objective
- lifecycle state
- optional human approval

AI may propose opportunities. Approval policy determines whether an opportunity can automatically become a campaign or production request.

## 7. Campaign objectives

Every Campaign has an explicit primary objective. Initial canonical objectives:

- ORGANIC_REACH
- ENGAGEMENT
- TRAFFIC
- LEAD_GENERATION
- APP_INSTALL
- PURCHASE
- BOOKING
- SEO_AUTHORITY
- RETENTION
- EXPERIMENT

Objectives determine optimization metrics. Views alone are never the universal north-star metric.

## 8. Content portfolio and series

The strategy layer may balance content by role, including discovery, value, authority, engagement, conversion, retargeting and experimental content.

A Content Series is a durable creative container with reusable format rules, continuity, characters, opening grammar, CTA policy and platform strategy. Episodes/items belong to a series instead of being isolated generations when recurring structure is intended.

## 9. Experiment model

The factory must represent a hypothesis explicitly rather than infer all learning after publication.

An experiment may vary one or more controlled dimensions such as:

- hook
- opening frame
- message angle
- CTA
- duration
- visual style
- speaker/character
- platform treatment
- publish timing

Results must remain tied to variant, audience, campaign, publication and objective.

## 10. Funnel model

Content may be connected to a measurable funnel:

```text
Content Item
  -> Publication
  -> CTA
  -> Destination
  -> Landing/Store/Conversation
  -> Lead
  -> Conversion
```

Funnels may contain multiple steps and may differ by product, market or audience.

## 11. Attribution

The factory must preserve attribution evidence where available, including campaign, content item, publication, platform, creative variant, destination and tracking identifiers such as UTM parameters.

Attribution confidence and model must be explicit. The system must not fabricate deterministic attribution when evidence is incomplete.

## 12. Performance events

Analytics should be event-oriented and append-oriented. Typical measurements include impressions, views, watch time, retention, engagement, clicks, sessions, leads, installs, bookings, purchases, revenue and cost.

Raw observations are historical evidence. Aggregates may be recomputed and must not destroy underlying observations.

## 13. Economic intelligence

Provider and production cost should be captured so outcomes can be evaluated economically. Examples:

- token/model cost
- image/video/audio generation cost
- storage/render cost
- cost per content item
- cost per lead
- acquisition cost
- revenue and ROI where available

Provider routing may use quality, capability, availability, latency and cost, but business metrics do not bypass quality or safety gates.

## 14. Creative memory and fatigue

The factory must be able to detect repeated concepts and creative fatigue. Creative memory may track semantic similarity, repeated hooks, repeated structures, character usage and prior performance.

Novelty is a decision signal, not an unconditional requirement: proven formats may intentionally be reused while stale repetition should be detectable.

## 15. Human decision layer

Approval is policy-driven. Canonical modes:

- AUTO
- REVIEW
- MANDATORY_APPROVAL

Approval policy may depend on brand, campaign, operation, cost threshold, claims risk, paid-media use or publication destination.

## 16. Provider independence

Growth-domain records must never depend directly on NVIDIA, OpenAI, Anthropic or another vendor. Provider selection remains behind the V2.1 provider gateway/adapter boundary.

## 17. Integration with V2.1 production

V2.2 adds business context to productions through explicit nullable foreign keys during migration compatibility. Existing V2.1 productions remain valid.

Target production context includes:

```text
workspace_id
brand_id
product_id
campaign_id
content_item_id
objective
```

Production execution state remains owned by V2.1 tables/functions.

## 18. Data principles

1. IDs are durable UUID identities.
2. Operational evidence is append-oriented where practical.
3. Brand boundaries are explicit.
4. Destructive overwrites of historical evidence are avoided.
5. Business context and execution state remain separate.
6. External-source claims preserve provenance/evidence references.
7. Secrets never belong in growth records or artifact provenance.
8. Schema changes are migration-driven and independently certified.

## 19. Delivery order

V2.2 implementation should proceed in this order:

1. growth ownership foundation: brands, products, markets, audiences, offers, funnels, campaigns;
2. safe linkage from V2.1 productions to growth context;
3. Brand Brain/versioned knowledge;
4. opportunity/evidence ingestion;
5. content series/items and experiment contracts;
6. distribution/publication linkage;
7. performance events and attribution;
8. lead/conversion and economic metrics;
9. strategy learning and creative memory;
10. approval policy expansion;
11. end-to-end certification.

A layer is not complete until implemented, tested and certified.

## 20. Non-goals

V2.2 does not turn the database into a vendor-specific marketing platform, does not hard-code one brand, does not optimize only for views, and does not grant autonomous publication or spending authority without policy.
